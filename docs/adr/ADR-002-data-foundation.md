# ADR-002 — Data foundation: real open data over a fully synthetic dataset

## 1. Context

The initial specification called for a **fully synthetic dataset**: roughly 500 real-estate assets generated by a Python script, taken as a starting assumption.

Verifying what is actually available as open data published under the French Open Licence invalidates that assumption. Two findings drive the move to real data.

**First finding — a quality test on self-generated data is circular.** If the generator never produces a null value, a `not_null` test always passes: it does not test the data, it tests the generator. A quality dashboard that stays green proves nothing. Three of the ten items in the v1.0 core concern data quality.

**Second finding — governance would be decorative.** Assigning owners and stewards to entities you invented yourself produces a screenshot, not a demonstration. The single most important object in the project — ownership — would be the most artificial one.

Seven options, all verified by direct calls to their APIs or files on **2026-08-26**. The volumes and completeness rates below are measured, not quoted from documentation.

## 3. Criteria

| Criterion | Weight | Rationale for the weight |
|---|---:|---|
| **Fit with the domain being demonstrated** | 25 % | The thesis is about governing a public real-estate portfolio. A dataset from another domain would force an alien vocabulary onto the data. |
| **Ability to carry governance objects** — owners, managers, scope of responsibility | 25 % | Ownership is the DMBOK object hardest to simulate and the most closely scrutinised. If it is invented, the demonstration collapses at the first probing question. |
| **Versionability of an extract** — 5 to 20 MB target in the repository | 20 % | Hard constraint inherited from ADR-001: no network call at startup, `docker compose up` and it runs. |
| **Richness of real data-quality defects** | 15 % | These are what make the tests non-circular. A dataset that is too clean does not demonstrate quality, it stages it. |
| **Joinability with Géorisques** | 10 % | Second dataset of the project; requires a shared geographic identifier. |
| **Licence compatible with MIT redistribution** | 5 % | Eliminatory but rarely discriminating: nearly all French open data uses the Open Licence. |

## 4. Evaluation

### Selected option — A, French State real-estate portfolio

Three published vintages, measured on 2026-08-26:

| Vintage | Rows | CSV |
|---|---:|---:|
| 2021-12-31 | 110,906 | ~35 MB |
| 2022-12-31 | **16,895** | **6.31 MB** |
| 2023-12-31 | **17,007** | **6.35 MB** |

**29 columns**, including those that directly carry the project's objects:

| v1.0 core requirement | Column(s) |
|---|---|
| Energy intensity KPI, kWh/m² | `consommation_kwh_ef`, `surface_m2` |
| Owners | `ministere`, `libelle_proprietaire`, `type_proprietaire` |
| Stewards | `libelle_gestionnaire`, `type_gestionnaire` |
| Géorisques join | `code_insee`, `latitude`, `longitude` |
| Asset-management attributes | `etat_de_sante`, `erp`, `type_de_chauffage`, `annee_de_construction`, `fonction`, `type` |
| Historisation | `date_de_reference` plus three vintages |

### Measured completeness — the heart of the decision

| Measure | 2021 | 2023 |
|---|---:|---:|
| `surface_m2` populated | 99.8 % | 100 % |
| `code_insee` populated | 27.9 % | 53.2 % |
| `consommation_kwh_ef` populated | **8.7 %** | **22.4 %** |
| **KPI computable** (consumption AND surface > 0) | **8.0 %** | **20.5 %** |
| KPI **and** Géorisques join both possible | 8,491 rows | 2,985 rows |
| `annee_de_construction = "0001"` (sentinel value) | **14.3 %** | 4.7 % |
| `etat_de_sante` populated | 12.5 % | 42.0 % |

**This incompleteness is treated as an asset, not a defect.** A Data Office's first deliverable at a client site is not a green dashboard: it is a quantified completeness assessment — *"78 % of your portfolio has no energy consumption figure; here is the exact rate per ministry, measured and tracked."* No generated dataset can produce that finding, because a generator always fills its columns.

Three further anomalies, observed and exploitable:

1. **Redaction of Ministry of Defence assets** — scrambled identifiers, blanked address, municipality and coordinates, label replaced by *"Bien contrôlé par le ministère des Armées"*. The publisher applies a confidentiality policy that is **visible in the data**. The classification demonstration documents a real policy instead of simulating one.
2. **Scope break between vintages** — 110,906 rows in 2021, 16,895 in 2022. A gap of that magnitude within the same reference dataset is exactly what a Data Office must detect, explain and trace.
3. **`annee_de_construction` typed as text**, with `"0001"` used as a filler value. A textbook typing defect to fix in the *silver* layer.

### Rejected options

| Option | Reason for rejection |
|---|---|
| **B — ADEME energy performance certificates** | Technically the strongest runner-up: 15.4 million rows, 230 fields, server-side filtering allowing a 7 MB extract, and a publisher-supplied `score_ban` confidence indicator. **Rejected on domain**: it describes private housing, not a public portfolio, and **carries no ownership or management information** — owners would have remained invented, which is the very defect this decision sets out to correct. |
| **C — Geolocated DVF** | Clean, split by municipality. Rejected: it describes **transactions**, not a portfolio; several rows per sale with multi-valued lots, so any naive aggregation double-counts; and **no fine-grained join key** with the retained datasets. |
| **D — BDNB** | The richest in building attributes. **Rejected on volume**: 36.7 GB for the national CSV. Incompatible with a repository a reader must be able to clone, even at département level. |
| **E — RPLS** | Social housing: out of domain. National CSV above 700 MB, and the DIDO API imposes an undocumented filter syntax (`?DEP_CODE=eq:33`; `?DEP_CODE=33` returns HTTP 400). |
| **F — OPERAT** | Ruled out despite strong thematic relevance — regulated tertiary-sector consumption, 1,093,038 rows: the actual schema **contains no geographic identifier**, no municipality, no INSEE code, no coordinates. A Géorisques join is impossible. Direct consequence: the glossary term *"OPERAT liability"* planned in the initial scope is dropped. |
| **G — Fully synthetic dataset** | Circular quality tests, decorative ownership, no completeness assessment possible. Would remain the right choice had no open dataset covered the domain — which is not the case. |
| *State property inventory (2014)* | Distinct from the selected dataset and **abandoned by its publisher**: last modified 2022-02-09, `date_inventaire` at March 2014, 13 columns, addresses overwhelmingly null. Noted here because its name invites confusion with option A. |

## 5. Decision

**Foundation**: the **French State real-estate portfolio, vintages 2022-12-31 and 2023-12-31** — 33,902 rows, 29 columns, **12.66 MB**, captured as versioned extracts in the repository.

**Second dataset**: a **Géorisques snapshot**, joined on `code_insee`. The v1 API was verified live on 2026-08-26, requires no token, and is capped at 1,000 requests per minute. It is captured as a frozen snapshot, per the principle set in ADR-001: no network call at startup.

**Synthetic overlay**: the only fabricated fields are the **contact fields** — steward name, email — absent from open data by construction and required to demonstrate PII classification. They are **flagged as synthetic in the catalog itself**, together with the reason.

**The 2021 vintage is not loaded** in v1.0: ~35 MB exceeds the versionability target. Its scope break against 2022 is recorded in `ROADMAP.md`.

### On personal data

Checked across all 29 columns: **no personal data**. `libelle_gestionnaire` and `libelle_proprietaire` denote administrative entities (`DEFENSE - BDD : PHALSBOURG`, `ETAT DOMAINE PUBLIC`), not natural persons.

The project adopts a **two-tier classification** rather than a single PII tag:

| Tier | Fields | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| `PII` | fabricated contact fields | Personal data under GDPR — synthetic, hence no legal exposure |
| `Confidential` | `adresse`, `latitude`, `longitude`, `designation_site` | The publisher redacts these itself on defence sites: sensitivity is established by the source |

The distinction is deliberate: it sets up the question of re-identification through cross-referencing, which the CNIL addresses in its June 2024 recommendations to open-data publishers.

### Attribution

Open Licence / Etalab 2.0 for both datasets — no share-alike clause, no incompatibility with the repository's MIT licence. The single obligation is attribution, carried by the `README` together with the extraction date.

## 6. Consequences

### Favourable

- Owners and stewards become **verifiable facts** rather than inventions.
- Quality tests stop being circular: they rest on measured defects — 14.3 % aberrant dates, 78 % missing consumption figures.
- The energy intensity KPI is computed **directly** from source columns.
- Confidentiality classification documents a **real policy**, applied by the publisher.
- Historisation rests on published vintages, not simulated ones.
- Significant time saved compared with building a synthetic data generator.

### Accepted downsides

- **The data dates from 2022 and 2023.** These are the most recent published vintages, released 2024-12-20, and `date_de_reference` is a column of the dataset: the lag is explicit and verifiable, not concealed.
- **Only ~3,000 assets feed the complete KPI** on the 2023 vintage. That is the reference dataset's real completeness rate. It is measured and displayed rather than worked around.
- The repository carries ~13 MB of data. Acceptable, but adding a third vintage would push it past the target.
- A synthetic component remains on the contact fields. The seam is documented in the catalog — an act of provenance traceability, not a concealed compromise.

## 7. Review triggers

This ADR should be revisited if:

- a 2024 or 2025 vintage of the State real-estate portfolio is published — the time lag would shrink without changing the decision;
- `consommation_kwh_ef` completeness proves too low to feed a legible *gold* KPI — documented fallback: switch the headline KPI to surface area by function, whose measured completeness is 100 %;
- a need arises for fine-grained building-to-parcel lineage — BDNB or RNB would become relevant again, at the cost of the volume constraint.
